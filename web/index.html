How to interpret the scenarios

A rising rate generally reduces a long fixed-rate bond’s price. Longer-duration positions typically move more.

A steepener can leave the front of the curve unchanged while increasing long rates. Matching total DV01 alone does not eliminate this exposure.

Each scenario discounts the same remaining cash flows at the shocked curve. Calibrated spreads remain fixed unless a spread shock is explicitly applied.

No time passes in these instantaneous shocks: coupon carry, rolldown, defaults, recovery and trading costs are excluded.

MODEL BOUNDARIES

Conventions that matter

Supported: regular fixed-rate bullet cash flows in USD, Actual/Actual coupon accrual, annual/semiannual/quarterly frequency, maturities up to approximately 30 years.

Curve construction: linear interpolation of par yields on a semiannual grid, sequential discount-factor bootstrap, then log-linear discount-factor interpolation. This is our approximation, not the Treasury’s official curve construction.

Settlement: tenor rates are applied at your selected settlement date. No automatic holiday adjustment or forecast of the curve between observation and settlement.

Unsupported: callable/putable bonds, amortizing bonds, odd coupons, ex-coupon periods, TIPS, floaters, default modeling, Treasury bill discount-yield conventions and broker execution.

Use: transparent valuation and scenario research. Check terms and reconcile against a trusted price source before relying on the result.
